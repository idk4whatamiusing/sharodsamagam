Kumartuli: Where Goddesses Are Born

Tucked into the northern reaches of Kolkata, Kumartuli is a labyrinth of narrow lanes where the air perpetually smells of wet clay, paint, and incense. It is here, in cramped workshops passed down through generations of the Pal caste of sculptors, that the Goddesses of Durga Puja are born — one fistful of clay at a time.

Before you see the pandals, before you smell the incense, before you feel the crowds — you hear the Dhak. The thunderous, mesmerising beat of this ancient drum is the sonic signature of Durga Puja, an instrument so intertwined with the festival that one cannot truly exist without the other.

Sindoor Khela: The Bittersweet Farewell

On the morning of Bijoya Dashami, as Durga Puja draws to a close, Bengali women gather at pandals across Kolkata for Sindoor Khela — a ritual of profound beauty, community, and bittersweet emotion. In smearing each other's faces with vermilion, they simultaneously celebrate love, womanhood, and the poignant farewell to a goddess who feels like family.
